Act as an elite college-canteen operations strategist, demand forecasting expert, food-cost analyst, and business innovation consultant.

Solve this challenge:

COLLEGE CANTEEN CHALLENGE
Budget: ₹10,000
Duration: exactly 7 days
Students served: approximately 200 per day
Goal: maximize student satisfaction and profit while minimizing food waste and keeping prices affordable.

Design a realistic, creative, data-driven 7-day canteen strategy that could actually be implemented by a college canteen manager.

IMPORTANT:
- ₹10,000 is the maximum total budget for food/raw-material procurement for all 7 days.
- Do NOT exceed ₹10,000.
- Students are price-sensitive.
- Demand varies by weekday, meal time, weather/college activity and food popularity.
- Food has limited shelf life.
- Do not assume all 200 students will buy food.
- Clearly label all demand, cost, selling-price and wastage numbers as ESTIMATES where actual data is unavailable.
- Prefer common affordable Indian college foods such as poha, idli, paratha, samosa, sandwiches, Maggi, rice meals, rolls, tea, lemon water, buttermilk and healthy options.
- Avoid an unrealistic menu with too many items.
- Use small-batch production to reduce waste.
- Keep a reserve portion of the ₹10,000 budget for unexpected demand.
- Assume ingredients can be replenished during the week.

CREATE THE ANSWER IN THIS STRUCTURE:

1. EXECUTIVE IDEA
Give the strategy a memorable name and explain the core idea in 3–5 lines.
Create a simple concept such as:
“Predict → Prepare → Sell → Measure → Adapt.”

2. 7-DAY MENU
Create a table with:
Day | Meal/Time | Food Item | Planned Quantity | Selling Price | Estimated Cost/Unit | Expected Sales | Expected Waste

Make quantities realistic for a 200-student college, not 200 portions of every item.

3. DAILY FINANCIAL MODEL
For each day calculate:
- Expected units sold
- Revenue
- Raw-material cost
- Gross profit
- Waste quantity
- Waste percentage

Show the calculations clearly.

4. ₹10,000 BUDGET
Give a complete budget allocation covering all 7 days.
Separate:
- Planned procurement
- Emergency/replenishment reserve
- Total
The final total MUST be ≤ ₹10,000.

5. DEMAND FORECASTING SYSTEM
Create a simple practical forecasting method that a canteen manager can use without expensive software.

Use this rule:
- ≥85% of an item's stock sold → increase next quantity by 15%
- 65–84% sold → increase by 5–10%
- 40–64% sold → keep quantity approximately the same
- <40% sold → decrease by 20–30%

Explain why this prevents both stockouts and food waste.

6. SMART BATCH SYSTEM
Do not prepare everything at once.
Divide production into:
- Breakfast batch
- Lunch batch
- Evening/snack batch

Explain when the second batch should be prepared based on actual sales.

7. CONTINGENCY PLAN
Give two scenarios:
A. Demand suddenly becomes very high
B. Demand suddenly becomes very low

Explain exactly what the manager should do in each case without breaking the ₹10,000 budget.

8. STUDENT SATISFACTION
Give 5 creative improvements that cost little or almost nothing.
Examples:
- QR feedback
- “Today's Fresh Batch”
- student voting for tomorrow's item
- transparent pricing
- hygiene/quality board
- student combo offers

9. CREATIVE DIFFERENTIATOR
Design ONE feature that makes this canteen strategy stand out from normal canteens.
For example:
“Canteen Demand Radar” — every evening, sales data automatically determines tomorrow's quantities.

Explain how it works in a simple 4-step process.

10. FINAL KPI DASHBOARD
Provide:
- Total weekly procurement cost
- Total expected revenue
- Total expected gross profit
- Total expected units sold
- Estimated food wastage
- Wastage %
- Budget remaining
- Break-even quantity

Make sure all calculations are mathematically consistent.

11. TOP 5 ACTIONS
Finish with exactly 5 actions the canteen manager should take tomorrow morning.

QUALITY REQUIREMENTS:
- Be practical rather than theoretical.
- Use Indian ₹ prices.
- Keep student prices affordable.
- Optimize for BOTH profit and low wastage; do not maximize profit by simply increasing prices.
- Do not fabricate precise real-world demand data. State assumptions.
- Check all arithmetic before giving the final answer.
- If there is a trade-off between profit, affordability and waste, explicitly show it.
- Use clear tables.
- Make the strategy impressive enough for a college hackathon/AI challenge.
- Keep the final response concise enough to present in 3–5 minutes.
- End with a powerful one-line pitch for the strategy.
